History of Monogon Industries

Founded 1964
Vocal8 → Monotape → MonoDisc → Monogon Industries

Monogon Industries began creating learning cartridges for audio computers in the early 1960's as Vocal8. Throughout the following decades Monogon pioneered early home computing hardware. After the console wars of the late 1980's, Monogon shifted focus towards Al designed, procedurally generated user operating systems primarily for edutainment purposes. Al operating research and development eventually lead to the early experiments in virtual interfacing systems. Monogon Industries and the VoidMind Al both strive to create an infinitely expanding virtual space for work, entertainment, education, and life; starting with MythOS City.

Tape era · 1964–1979 Optical era · 1980–1991 Simulation era · 1992–1997
Tape era1964–1979
1964

My Audio Pal

Becomes the most popular Christmas gift for children of all ages.

1965

Vocal8 dominates Stereo 8

Vocal8 (Monogon Ind.) distributes the greatest number of Stereo 8 tape cartridges in North America.

1968

Eight-track becomes standard

Eight-track magnetic tape becomes the preferred method of educational information storage for mass consumption.

1969

Apollo 11 transcoded to tape

Vocal8 makes history by converting magnetic tape audio data to short-form video, transcoding the Apollo 11 Moon landing.

1972–1973

Split into V8C and Monotape

Vocal8 begins experimentation with compact cassette tape, branching the company to create V8C and Monotape.

1977–1979

V8C's arcade gamble fails

V8C's decision to reject home arcade and computation units proves ill-fated as Monotape thrives instead, developing a hardware add-on for cassette-play integration.

Optical era1980–1991
1980

First disc-based readers

Monotape begins early development into disc-based laser readers.

1981–1982

Media Board of Canada phases out tape

The Media Board of Canada Group declares that magnetic tape media should be phased out of circulation in favor of a more stable information storage format.

1983

Monotape rebrands to MonoDisc

Rebranding preemptively distances the company from the magnetic tape format, preparing for a full transfer to optical media.

1984

Cassette media continues

MonoDisc continues to release cassette media, most notably audiobooks on tape.

1985–1986

Development of an edutainment computer

Work begins on an optical-format home entertainment and educational computer.

1988

The PolySystem launches

Holiday release of the first Monodisc home edutainment device, met with roaring success.

1990

PolySystem re-issue & OldMyth begins

PolySystem is re-issued and initial development begins on OldMyth. The same year, Monogon discovers the ████████.

1991

Old Myth OS releases

Old Myth releases as a home computing operating system add-on for the PolySystem.

Simulation era1992–1997
1992

Monodisc becomes Monogon Industries

Rebrands as Monogon Industries and heavily finances research into simulated reality, built primarily around successful experiments in the ████████.

1994

Prophet: the first wearable

Monogon launches the first wearable computing device, code-named Prophet (released as “The Hat” or simply “p1”). Running a virtual conversion of OldMyth: Classroom, it paves the way for a wave of computer headgear and the eventual development of MythOS.

1995–1996

Becomes a global conglomerate

Monogon Industries expands into telecommunications, hardware and software development, education philanthropy, nutritional sciences, medical research, energy conservation, and housing programs.

1997
🌐Planned Release — MythOS 1.0
MythOS City

Launch of MythOS City — an infinitely expanding virtual space for work, entertainment, education, and life.
